The real problem

A lead and a booked call worth taking are two different things.

When an account is optimized for cost per lead, the algorithm buys the cheapest clicks it can find, and the cheapest clicks are usually the least serious buyers. That is exactly how a small sales team ends up on calls that go nowhere. Here is where the tire-kickers get in.

Red flag

Optimizing to cost per lead rewards volume, not quality. Every form fill and click counts the same to the platform, so spend keeps flowing toward the wrong people while your team pays the price in wasted call time.

The 5 leak points that create tire-kicker leads

1
Loose keywords and broad match pull in info-seekers, price-shoppers, and people searching a completely different intent, and you pay for every one of them.

The strategy

A qualification system that repels tire-kickers before they ever reach your team.

This is not more targeting for the sake of targeting. Every layer below exists to do one job: send your sales team fewer, better calls, and teach the platform to buy more of them over time.

1

Query-level intent filtering

Full search-term audit, a heavy negative-keyword cleanup, and match types tightened so budget stops buying info-seekers and price-shoppers. The account only pays for searches that signal real buying intent.

2

Ad and landing page qualification

Copy that pre-frames the offer, sets price or eligibility expectations, and adds qualifying questions. This deliberately repels the wrong clicks and pulls forward the people ready to book.

3

Call-first tracking

Call-only and high-intent campaigns backed by call tracking, so a booked call becomes the conversion the account optimizes toward, not a raw form fill. You finally see which spend created real business.

4

Audience, geo and schedule concentration

Spend is pulled back to the locations, hours, and audiences that historically produce booked calls. Wasted impressions in low-value pockets get cut, exactly the move that drove cost per lead down 71% on a comparable account.

5

Sales-to-ads feedback loop

Each week your team tags which calls were worth taking. Those get imported back as offline conversions, so the platform learns to buy more booked calls and fewer tire-kickers. This is the layer most freelancers skip, and it is where the compounding gains live.
